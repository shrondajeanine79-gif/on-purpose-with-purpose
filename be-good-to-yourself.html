8 Ways to Be Good to Yourself

Pause. Tend. Become.

Being good to yourself can begin with a small choice. These eight invitations offer room to reflect, honor your needs, and find what feels possible on your own path.

Read the Guide

By Shronda Jeanine · On Purpose With Purpose · Free to read, no signup required.

A purpose of your own

Eight invitations. Your own pace.

You do not have to try everything at once. You might begin with one idea, return to another later, or adapt a practice to your circumstances. Let this be a place to listen to yourself.

  1. Set boundaries that protect your energy.

    Learn to say no to commitments that drain you, and don’t feel guilty about protecting your time and mental space.

    A moment to reflect: Where might a little more space support you?

  2. Talk to yourself like you’d talk to a friend.

    Notice when your inner voice becomes harsh or critical, and consciously shift to the kind of supportive language you’d use with someone you care about.

    A moment to reflect: What would you say to someone you love in this moment?

  3. Prioritize rest without justifying it.

    Sleep when you’re tired, take breaks during the day, and remember that rest is productive—it’s how your body and mind repair and recharge.

    A moment to reflect: What kind of rest feels possible for you today?

  4. Move your body in ways that feel good.

    This doesn’t mean punishing workouts. It could be dancing, stretching, walking in nature, or anything that makes you feel alive and connected to your physical self.

    A moment to reflect: What movement feels comfortable and accessible to you?

  5. Create small moments of pleasure.

    Whether it’s savoring your morning coffee, lighting a candle, listening to music you love, or sitting in the sun for a few minutes—intentionally build tiny joys into your routine.

    A moment to reflect: What small joy would you like to make room for?

  6. Let go of productivity guilt.

    You don’t have to earn the right to exist or relax. Your worth isn’t measured by your output.

    A moment to reflect: Who are you beyond what you get done?

  7. Seek help when you need it.

    Whether that’s talking to a friend, seeing a therapist, asking for support with tasks, or getting medical care—reaching out is a sign of strength and self-respect.

    A moment to reflect: Who might you feel comfortable reaching out to?

  8. Forgive yourself for being human.

    You’ll make mistakes, have bad days, and fall short of your own expectations sometimes. Treat these moments as part of the learning process rather than evidence of failure.

    A moment to reflect: What would a little more gentleness toward yourself look like?
